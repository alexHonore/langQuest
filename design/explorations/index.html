Personality

The current silhouette (floating pebble body, big eyes, mitten arms, one ribbon tail on the right) kept exactly, with everything inside it rebuilt: two line weights, a filled twisted ribbon tail, taller eyes with iris ring, five brows, eight mouths, ten arm gestures, a body that leans away from the tail and a faint hover shadow. Always just arriving next to you; curious before clever.

Scales: 24 dp icon keeps body + eyes + ribbon; full figure from 40 dp; dark mode is a token swap.

Strengths

  • Drop-in continuity: same silhouette, tail-on-the-right rule and 17-state vocabulary as the existing style guide and mascot.ts contract.
  • Widest expression kit of the five (6 lids × 5 brows × 8 mouths × 10 arms × continuous tail), all from one generate.js.
  • Reads at 24 px and in four grayscale values; every part has a stable pivot for Rive bones.

Risks

  • A teal blob with big eyes is a crowded genre; the ribbon tail is the only strong differentiator and must never be cropped.
  • Teal + coral sits close to several consumer-app palettes (not a copy, but little distance).
  • Tail at full lift grows the bounding box ~30 units upward; square slots must allow for it.

Personality

A floating lantern: translucent shell with rim, crown cap and loop, a warm teardrop of light inside whose level (off / dim / rest / bright / blaze) is the mood, two petal fin-ears that perk up when listening and fold when offline, and a spark that always trails lower-left. No limbs; meaning is carried by light, fin angle and face. Attentive, warm, a little dreamy.

Scales: Icon drops gradient and highlight, enlarges fins and spark; RTL mirrors the whole figure (spark then lower-right).

Strengths

  • Ownable metaphor for the product: light = understanding, mapped to real states (thinking = dim, success = bright) without faking evaluation.
  • Where dark mode shines: the glow does real work on dark canvases (see dark variant).
  • No limbs means few rig bones; most motion is scale / opacity / rotation, cheap on low-end Android.

Risks

  • Bird read. The two orange petal fins beside a rounded body can scan as wings on a chick; a hard rule of this brief. Needs a silhouette pass (fins lower, narrower, or ribbed) before approval.
  • Drifts toward egg / light-bulb if rim, cap or loop are dropped (the first draft read as a fried egg).
  • Fewer gestures: pointing and thumbs-up must be done with tilt, fins and props; translucency loses contrast on busy scenes.

Personality

A round, unhurried chameleon who adapts instead of judging: two big eyes on gentle turrets that swivel independently (one on you, one on the idea), a soft saffron crest, two flank bands, stubby mitten limbs and a spiral tail used as a gesture limb (wave, question-hook, upright cheer, droop). Body hue drifts per course family while crest, bands and outline stay fixed.

Scales: 24 px keeps body, crest, two turrets with pupils and the spiral; course tints only touch body/bodyShade tokens.

Strengths

  • Independent eyes are a trait nobody in the category owns and carry listening / thinking / curious for free.
  • Adaptation mechanic is built in: a six-tint body dial per course family while the silhouette stays the brand anchor.
  • Tiny files (states 4.7–6.2 KB), grounded pose, no scales / teeth / claws, so it reads as plush, not lizard.

Risks

  • "Frog with goggles" misread at a glance; the turret rings must stay close in tone to the body.
  • Reptile aversion in some audiences; the word "chameleon" may need to stay out of marketing copy in some locales.
  • Green is a common success colour; celebrate must rely on pose and confetti (it does), and hue shifts must never encode feedback.

Personality

A small cloud that got curious about the ground and walked down on stubby legs, wearing a hand-knitted cranberry scarf whose trailing end is the expressive tail, plus matching boots. Its feelings are its weather: a puff rises to wave, bulges into an ear cup to listen, speech-puffs drift off when it talks, one raindrop falls on a missed try, a sun peeks out on success.

Scales: Stitches drop below 48 dp (band becomes plain); dark token set uses a mid-indigo outline at ≥ 3:1.

Strengths

  • Emotion carried by shape (rain, sun, speech-puffs), not colour; weather metaphors read across cultures.
  • Cheap, charming motion without limbs or complex rigging; plush, low-contrast body stays calm over scene art.
  • Instant silhouette at 24 px: bumpy top, dark band, two feet, a tail.

Risks

  • Crowded category: cute clouds exist in stickers and weather apps; distinctiveness rests on scarf + boots + weather-as-emotion. Trademark search needed.
  • Rain can read as sadness; try_again must keep the soft smile and exactly one drop.
  • Lavender-white body needs its outline or a tinted card on pure white surfaces; no arms, so hints are pointed with a puff.

Personality

A creature folded from one sheet of indigo paper: a six-sided faceted pod lit from the top-left, a cream face window, and a visor flap above it that folds up or down and acts as the brow (high = welcome, half = thinking, closed = offline). A tangerine paper-plane dart at the right hip always points where the learner should go next (up = keep going, down toward the mic = your turn). Nothing is round except the eyes and smile.

Scales: Silhouette priority when simplifying: pod → dart → face window → brim → arm tabs.

Strengths

  • The most distinct silhouette of the five (hexagon + dart vs. every blob and bird on the market).
  • One mechanic that is also the brand story: unfolding = opening up and speaking; the dart is an honest UI affordance.
  • Polygons only, three fills at 24 px, four grayscale values; cheapest to animate (flap, dart, tabs).

Risks

  • Warmth ceiling: hard edges can read corporate or logo-like if the face is drawn small; keep the face window large.
  • Ink outline nearly vanishes on the dark canvas; silhouette relies on facet contrast (a light rim may be needed).
  • Half-closed flap crops the eyes; at 24 px thinking should use the flat mouth + notes cue instead.

2 · Character styles (C1–C4)

The same three cast members (Nadia, Kenji, Lucía) in the same four conversation states and the same crop, so the styles can be compared pose for pose, plus the Casablanca café vignette in each style's environment treatment. Clothing is contemporary everyday wear in every style; no flags, costumes, landmarks or real-person likeness.

C1

Cut paper, warm light — Geometric flat (refined baseline)

Kenji

Lucía

The style

The baseline's geometric DNA carried through properly: no outlines on fills (edges by luminance contrast, ≥ 0.12 between touching materials), exactly one flat shade per material from the top-left, line only for brows / closed mouth / glasses, solid eyes with one highlight that also carries gaze. One parametric head with per-character face proportions; hair and garment silhouettes do the characterisation.

Cost for 30 courses: ~2 weeks for a part library (12 hair, 15 garments, 10 accessories), then 1–2 h of art direction per character; scenes 0.5–1 day with day / evening / dark as tint swaps.

Strengths

  • Scale-proof and cheapest to produce: portraits are 3–6 KB, a new character is ~40 lines of parameters.
  • Honest continuity: current assets/characters migrate by removing strokes and adding one shade.
  • Warm, adult palette (seven-step skin ramp with matched blush, terracotta / teal / olive / mustard garments).

Risks

  • Can look generic; flat-geometric people are a crowded genre, so distinctiveness must come from silhouettes, palette and the mascot.
  • Low emotional range at small sizes (no sclera, no line work).
  • Lighting never changes per scene; evening is a background tint only.
Pairing notes with the five mascots
M1Good with one tweak: M1 keeps a 2.6 px silhouette line, C1 has none. Give M1 a no-line in-scene variant, or a silhouette-only outline flag on C1.
M2Strong contrast pairing: a glowing translucent guide against matte opaque people. Keep the halo as the only soft gradient on screen.
M3Natural fit (both flat rounded shapes); Melo's hue dial sits cleanly on fixed warm people. Watch leaf green vs. Kenji's olive cardigan.
M4Closest stylistic match, same cut-paper world; both so soft that CTAs need the stronger terracotta / teal tokens.
M5Thematic match, tonal tension: crisp angular Plio over round people. Works if creases stay at 45 % and facets use the one-shade rule.
C2

Soft dimensional — Clay / plush

Nadia
30s · café counter, Casablanca
Kenji
60s · neighbour, Tokyo
Lucía

The style

Characters as softly lit clay or plush figures: rounded inflated forms, a warm key light upper-left via 3-stop radial gradients, a cool clipped rim light lower-right, a small specular and soft contact shadows, zero outlines (shapes separate by value only). Glossy bead eyes, button nose, sculpted lips. One parametric rig drives brows, eye openness, gaze, mouth, tilt and hand.

Cost for 30 courses: Half a day of art direction per character once the rig is approved; 60–120 characters ≈ 30–60 artist-days; scenes 1–2 days each.

Strengths

  • Premium, warm, tactile; adults do not feel talked down to; photographs well in marketing.
  • Deliberately unlike the flat outlined saturated look of the category; well clear of any trade dress.
  • Environment uses the same shaded-form recipe so scenes and cast sit in one world.

Risks

  • Rendering cost: ~60 gradient elements per portrait, ~250 per scene; list thumbnails must be rasterised.
  • Mouths animate well as swaps, badly as morphs (plan 4–6 shapes).
  • Dark-mode variant and the 24 px read are the weakest of the four (gradients collapse to mush at tab size).
Pairing notes with the five mascots
M1Best if re-rendered with the exact C2 recipe (focal radial, cool rim, specular, contact shadow); it becomes a sibling of the cast.
M2Workable: M2 is already soft and lit; keep its glow as the only emissive object and give it a contact shadow.
M3Flat outlined shapes clash on the same screen; re-render with the C2 recipe or keep it strictly to UI chrome.
M4Good: a single inflated form takes the clay recipe with no redesign.
M5Geometric facets fight the inflated look; keep Plio to tab icons and toasts, never beside a portrait.

C2's README states pairing rules per mascot archetype; the lines above apply those rules to M1–M5.

C3

Ink & Fill — "Carnet" — hand-drawn travel sketchbook

Nadia
30s · café counter, Casablanca
Kenji
60s · neighbour, Tokyo
Lucía

The style

A true variable-width ribbon renderer: every line is a tapered filled stroke, heavier on the shadow side and under the chin, with wobble seeded per feature; fills are flat and deliberately misregistered from the ink (+1.6 / +1.3), one hard shadow and at most three hatch lines per area, warm near-black ink on cream paper. The people you meet, drawn quickly and warmly by someone sitting across the table.

Cost for 30 courses: A new character ≈ 60 lines of parameters; ~1–2 person-days per 10 characters once hair / garment blocks are reused; scenes ship as WebP (40–80 KB) with SVG kept as source.

Strengths

  • Genuinely different drawing language from the baseline and from every big language app.
  • Forgiving of production variance: wobble and misregistration are the family traits, so a cast across 30 courses can grow without geometric sameness.
  • Warm, adult, editorial; sits well next to real café / street photography if media ever mix.

Risks

  • Heaviest files (44–67 KB per portrait, ~125 KB scene) and the ink collapses to a halo at 24 px; icons need a 1.6× ink regeneration.
  • Hard shadows can read as marks on skin if too dark or narrow.
  • Tone mismatch with a glossy mascot; the guide must be a different pen or a different material, decided, not accidental.
Pairing notes with the five mascots
M1Works after re-inking: body outline at 4.0, tail as one tapered ribbon (it already is one), drop belly gradient and glossy highlight.
M2Clash unless deliberate: either flatten and ink it, or keep it glossy as the only un-inked object, always on its own paper-white badge.
M3Flat vector: re-ink with the C3 pen at silhouette weight, add paper-tint misregistration, keep its shapes.
M4Same as M3; the knit stitches become hatch lines naturally.
M5Abstract / geometric: ink the outline only and let tangerine be the single saturated element. Strong guide-vs-interlocutor separation.

README · style sheet · café vignette · portraits/

Nadia
30s · café counter, Casablanca
Kenji
60s · neighbour, Tokyo
Lucía
20s · coworker, Madrid

The style

Large simplified shapes with no outlines, two-tone faces (one light tone plus a single curved shade plane, nose as a shade wedge), ink-only brows / eyes / mouth, one bold hair silhouette per character, and exactly one gradient per figure: the backing disc (terracotta→saffron for Nadia, teal→sage for Kenji, plum→coral for Lucía). Editorial poster art rather than cartoon.

Cost for 30 courses: 1–2 hours per new lead; the full ~16-character roster in two to three working days; 30 courses × 2–3 leads in one to two weeks.

Strengths

  • Most legible at small sizes: five distinct grayscale values per avatar, nothing thinner than 3 px on 256.
  • Tiny files (2.7–3.8 KB) and trivial rendering; lip-sync is a mouth-shape swap.
  • Hue carries identity, value carries legibility, so dark mode and colour-blind checks are predictable.

Risks

  • Faces can read as corporate flat unless each character gets a signature hair silhouette, prop and gradient pair.
  • Two-tone split needs per-character skin pairs on dark skin tones (never derived by darkening).
  • Many gradient discs side by side look loud in lists; use the cream-disc variant there.
Pairing notes with the five mascots
M1Good: the outlined guide vs. the cut-out world helps roles read. Tie them by setting M1's outline to C4 ink #1F1D2B.
M2Strongest pairing after M5: M2's glow is itself a single gradient, so the one-gradient rule applies to both.
M3Workable, but the hue-shift body plus per-character discs can clash in the gallery; give the mascot the cream disc.
M4Good tonal fit; keep the scarf tail width ≥ the C4 brow weight so both share one stroke scale.
M5Best stylistic match: faceted planes are the same language as C4's shade plane; treat crease lines as C4 ink.

3 · UI directions (U1–U4)

Home, lesson (listen → anticipate → speak) and live conversation, light and dark side by side. Every direction shows the honest patterns the spec requires: no pronunciation score where none is measured, a reconnecting state with the mic disabled and "nothing is being recorded", speech indicators that only move while audio really flows, Arabic RTL and CJK samples. Tokens pages list both palettes with computed WCAG ratios.

U1

Warm Editorial — A well-set magazine, not a game console

README · tokens (palettes, contrast ratios, type, radii, motion)

Each phone is the real mockup file in an iframe (stage 500×960 scaled to 44 %). Pages that carry a side sheet or a second reduced phone are cropped here; use open to see the whole page. Mockups inline the current baseline mascot, characters and scenes as placeholders so the comparison is about the UI, not the art.

The direction

Cream paper surfaces (#F6F0E6 light / #121819 dark), deep teal as the single "you can act on this" colour, coral reserved for warmth and progress (never small white text), a system serif display face for the greeting, mission title and the character's target-language line, 20 px cards with soft ink-tinted shadows in light and hairline elevation in dark.

Strengths

  • Calm, adult, premium; two accents only, both borrowed from the mascot, so mascot + UI feel like one system.
  • Dark mode is a genuine design (hairline elevation, cream ink, lifted teal / coral) with higher contrast than light.
  • All text pairs ≥ 4.5:1 computed at build time; the only sub-AA pair (white on coral) is documented as disallowed.

Risks

  • Serif availability varies across Android OEM skins; may need a sans display fallback.
  • Soft 6–8 % shadows can vanish on cheap LCDs, flattening cards.
  • Calm may read as quiet: celebration energy depends on Rive / Lottie and the mascot.

Motion plan: Rive for the mascot (one instance per screen, 17 contract states, idle ±2 %, blink ~4 s, reaction ≤ 400 ms). Lottie for chapter-complete and achievement reveals (≤ 900 ms, skippable). Reanimated for press scale, hint sheet, meter fill and the mission-card → lesson shared element. Audio indicators are bound to real recorder / player signals; reduced motion pauses Rive on neutral and zeroes durations.

U2

Night Market — A conversation happening under lights

README · tokens (palettes, contrast ratios, type, radii, motion)

Each phone is the real mockup file in an iframe (stage 500×960 scaled to 44 %). Pages that carry a side sheet or a second reduced phone are cropped here; use open to see the whole page. Mockups inline the current baseline mascot, characters and scenes as placeholders so the comparison is about the UI, not the art.

The direction

Dark-first: a deep indigo canvas lit by three blurred lantern accents (magenta, amber, cyan) behind glassy translucent cards. Glow is rationed to exactly two things, the speech state (who is talking, whether the mic is open) and the one primary action per screen; everything else is quiet glass. The light variant is derived from the same tokens.

Strengths

  • Distinctive and premium at a glance; nothing like the flat-green / flat-blue competitor set.
  • Glow carries real meaning (speech state, primary action) so the speak-first loop is legible from across the room.
  • Dark-first suits when people actually practise speaking (evening, transit) and saves OLED battery.

Risks

  • Backdrop blur is expensive on low-end Android; needs a solid-card glassFallback tier.
  • The light variant is the weaker sibling; the owner should decide whether light is first-class.
  • A souk / lantern theme applied to all 30 courses frames every language through one region's imagery; the README does not flag this, I do (see matrix).

Motion plan: Rive for the mascot (idle / blink, listening lean, reaction bounce, connection_error shake → settle). Lottie for confetti and achievement reveals (≤ 1.2 s). Reanimated owns the speech glow (shared value from the audio engine), mic press spring (damping 14 / stiffness 180), card entrance, tab cross-fade, banner slide and a 22–31 s lantern drift that is disabled on low-end devices and under reduced motion.

U3

Paper & Ink — A travel journal you speak into

README · tokens (palettes, contrast ratios, type, radii, motion)

Each phone is the real mockup file in an iframe (stage 500×960 scaled to 44 %). Pages that carry a side sheet or a second reduced phone are cropped here; use open to see the whole page. Mockups inline the current baseline mascot, characters and scenes as placeholders so the comparison is about the UI, not the art.

The direction

Warm near-white paper (#F8F4EA) with one ink (#1B1A17) and one bright cobalt accent (#1F49E0, the ballpoint on a postcard), system serif for titles, platform sans for everything else, and original hand-drawn marks: a wobbly underline under what matters, dashed rubber stamps for dates and states, tape strips holding scene photos, a pen-circled mic, a tally-mark speaking meter. Dark mode is real night paper (#151410), not an inversion.

Strengths

  • Cannot be mistaken for Duolingo, Babbel, Memrise or Drops; no gradient cards, no progress rings.
  • Honesty is built into the visual language: stamps carry words, meters are tally marks, states are labelled pills.
  • Scripts look best here: ink/paper 15.8:1, Arabic, CJK and Devanagari sit on calm paper like the real thing.

Risks

  • Can read quiet or serious if doodles are under-used; needs a decoration budget per screen.
  • The baseline mascot is the loudest colour on the page; the chosen mascot needs an ink-and-paper rendition.
  • Serif below 16 px is unreadable; paper grain on OLED must be capped and device-tested.

Motion plan: Rive for the mascot only. Lottie for the pen-circled mic ring draw-in and the stamp thump on goal reached (≤ 420 ms). Reanimated for everything structural: underline draw-in, button press (offset shadow collapses), speech-state dot pulse, card enter stagger, page transitions (140 / 220 / 420 ms). Nothing animates while nothing is happening; the reconnecting state has no motion at all.

U4

Soft Clay — Pastel tiles you can press

README · tokens (palettes, contrast ratios, type, radii, motion)

Each phone is the real mockup file in an iframe (stage 500×960 scaled to 44 %). Pages that carry a side sheet or a second reduced phone are cropped here; use open to see the whole page. Mockups inline the current baseline mascot, characters and scenes as placeholders so the comparison is about the UI, not the art.

The direction

Every card and button is a clay tile: pastel fill, 28 / 22 / 16 px radii, a 2 px inner highlight on top and a 4 px inner shadow at the bottom, plus a 160 ms spring on press. Five pastels carry roles (peach = speak, mint = progress and character speaking, lilac = course / review, butter = mascot / warnings, sky = audio) with dark ink on all of them; one deep coral is reserved for the single microphone / Speak tab.

Strengths

  • Depth is carried by edges, so it survives grayscale, low-end screens and 24 px icons; three box-shadows per tile.
  • One contrast rule (dark ink on pastel, white only on deep coral) keeps every pair predictable; ink on peach 9.9:1.
  • The tactile mic is the most prominent object on every speaking screen.

Risks

  • Pastel clay tips into kids-app territory if saturation or radii creep; guardrails required.
  • Inset shadows are not native in React Native (two-layer view or 9-slice).
  • Tertiary text sits at ~3–3.5:1 and must stay restricted to 11 px bold labels next to higher-contrast elements.

Motion plan: Rive for the mascot state machine (idle / blink, listening, speaking, thinking, celebrate, error_recovery). Lottie for confetti and medal reveals (≤ 1.2 s). Reanimated springs {damping 14, stiffness 220} for press and pop-in, 420 ms timing for settle and meters, a mic ring bound to the real input level; speech-chip bars loop only while character audio is really playing.

5 · Suggested combinations

Three coherent pairings. Any other M + C + U is possible; these are the ones where the three parts argue for each other.

A · The journal M5 + C3 + U3

most original, most adult

One material story end to end: a mascot folded from paper, people drawn in ink on paper, a UI that is paper. C3's own rule (the guide must be a different pen from the cast) is satisfied for free because Plio is crisp and faceted while the cast is wobbly ribbon ink, so guide and interlocutor never blur. Tangerine dart + cobalt accent are the only two saturated colours on screen. Risk to manage: all three lean quiet; give Plio the warm, generous face from its model sheet and spend U3's decoration budget on celebration moments.

B · The warm evolution M1 + C1 + U1

lowest risk, fastest to ship

Everything already in the repo migrates: same silhouette and state contract for the mascot, strokes removed and one shade added on the current characters, the existing teal / coral tokens refined. The three were literally designed around each other (C1 pairs M1 with a no-line variant; U1 tunes its two accents to M1's body and ribbon). Cheapest for 30 courses. Risk to manage: least differentiated from the category; distinctiveness must come from motion and from a strict ribbon-tail-always-visible rule.

C · The night market M2 + C4 + U2

most striking, dark-first

C4 names M2 its strongest pairing after M5 because both obey a one-gradient rule (the disc, the glow), and U2's glow language gives Glim's light levels a real job: the mascot's core brightness and the speech ring share one token. Poster cut-outs stay legible under scrims on dark glass. Risks to manage: M2 needs a silhouette pass so the fins never read as wings; U2 needs its solid-card fallback for low-end Android and a decision on whether the light theme is first-class; and the lantern framing should be made course-neutral (tint lanterns from each course's cover) so it does not read as one region's décor.

Provenance. All mascot and character artwork is original vector work drawn in code for these explorations; no traced, stock, AI-generated or CC0 material (Open Peeps, Open Doodles, unDraw) was used in any folder, including as decoration. UI mockups inline the repository's current baseline assets as placeholders. No fonts are embedded anywhere; this page and all sheets use the system font stack.

Checks run for this gallery. Every SVG under mascots/, characters/ and ui/ validates as standalone XML (xmllint), has xmlns and viewBox, has no external reference and is under 200 KB; every HTML mockup loads no external resource. Every path referenced from this page was verified to exist at build time.
